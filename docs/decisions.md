| D-19 | Amplifier is not global. It only multiplies the speaker's loudness, which extends range through the normal distance rolloff. Earshot ranks peers by *perceived loudness* (gain / distance^rolloff), not raw distance, so a loud speaker is heard from further away without a special case. | DECIDED 2026-09-09 | Peter: "Amplification is not global, just increases the loudness (and therefore range)." Supersedes the "always in earshot, gentler rolloff" parts of D-06 and D-08. Gain value to tune in Phase 2 (start 4×). |
| D-20 | Closed rooms cut audibility. A world may define rooms as axis-aligned boxes with a `closed` flag; a peer inside a closed room is audible only to peers inside the same room, and hears only them. Derived locally from poses; nothing new crosses the network. | DECIDED 2026-09-09 | Peter: "Closed rooms cut off audibility outside." Hard cut for the PoC; wall attenuation and doors are later work. |
| D-21 | Phase 1 starts with a **locally stored** world of primitives (`public/worlds/shapes/world.json`: boxes, pyramids, a cylinder, a sphere) instead of a glTF scene on Swarm. Spikes S1, S6, S7 run inside the app's debug panel (gear icon or `?panel`) rather than as separate throwaway folders. S2–S5 stay as separate spikes before Phase 2. | DECIDED 2026-09-09 | Peter: "start with the basic visual navigation on a locally stored simple world of a few 3D shapes." The manifest keeps `scene` and `objects` fields so Swarm loading and glTF slot in later without a schema change. |
| D-22 | Steering modes for the D-10 feel test: `look` (default; heading = view yaw, go where you look), `yawrate` (phone yaw relative to a body reference is a handlebar angle; heading turns at a proportional rate; recentre sets the reference), `roll` (bank to steer, look freely). Switchable in the debug panel. Recentre is a double-tap or the panel button, not a long press, because holding is the brake. Recentre only changes yaw (gravity fixes pitch and roll) and never moves the view: the vehicle heading absorbs the offset. | PROPOSED 2026-09-09 | In `look` and `roll` modes recentre has nothing to reference and is inert; only `yawrate` needs it. D-10 is decided after Peter tries all three on the phone. |
| D-23 | Screen size estimate: 1 CSS px = 1/160 in (Android dp), times a calibration factor stored in `localStorage`, set by matching an on-screen bar to a bank card's long edge (85.6 mm). Viewing distance 40 cm. | PROPOSED 2026-09-09 | S7 decides whether calibration must always be asked for. |
| D-24 | Throttle by **displacement**: speed is proportional to how far the phone is held from a base position along the screen normal (joystick in space), full speed at 12 cm, 1.5 cm deadband, pull back for reverse. Displacement comes from integrating linear acceleration with zero-velocity updates when the phone is still and slow leaks to bound drift. Holding the screen brakes and re-bases. The impulse model of design §4.3 stays selectable as `impulse`. | PROPOSED 2026-09-09 | Peter, after the first phone test: "motion is slow (hard to even notice). It should be easier to start too. Shouldn't speed be proportional to distance from base position?" Supersedes the throttle part of D-10 if confirmed. Motion source order: `LinearAccelerationSensor`, `devicemotion.acceleration`, `accelerationIncludingGravity` minus gravity from the orientation quaternion. |
| D-25 | Vanadium blocks the "Motion sensors" site permission by default; the app shows a banner with the reasons and instructions when no orientation source delivers readings. | DECIDED 2026-09-09 | Found in the first Pixel 7a test. Record in S1. |
| D-26 | Default world is a real city fragment: about 1.1 km around the Eiffel Tower, generated offline by `scripts/make-world.mjs` from OpenStreetMap via Overpass (building footprints extruded by `height` or `building:levels`, default 19 m; roads as ribbons; parks and the Seine as flat areas). The tower is a procedural model from published dimensions because OSM has only its footprint. The result is a static `world.json` in the bundle; the phone never contacts Overpass (D-02). Attribution "© OpenStreetMap contributors (ODbL)" is in the manifest and on the start screen. | DECIDED 2026-09-09 | Peter: "replace the space with a small part of a city for which buildings are available in 3D and is generally well known. Paris close to the Eiffel tower?" Supersedes the "few landmarks" part of D-12 for the default world; the `shapes` world stays for tests. No collision with buildings in the PoC. |
| D-27 | Eye distance from the front camera: iris diameter (11.7 mm) as the primary cue, interpupillary distance as a cross-check, one-time focal-length calibration, MediaPipe Face Landmarker at 5–10 inferences/s, with the inertial displacement estimator bridging between fixes. The measured eye position replaces the fixed 40 cm and drives an off-axis frustum: the window widens as the eye approaches, geometry stays exact (D-05). | PROPOSED 2026-09-09 | Peter: "being close to the porthole should open up a much wider view, without breaking the geometry." Spike S8 first. Brings "face tracking" forward from design §10 to Phase 1c. Web pages get no depth, proximity or lens-position data, so the camera is the only absolute source. Battery is explicitly not a concern now; inference rate is a setting. |
| D-28 | Open: once eye distance is measured, whether the throttle stays inertial displacement (D-24) or becomes eye distance. Both read "push the phone away"; the second has no drift but couples speed to window width. | OPEN | Decide after S8. |
| D-29 | Viewpoint eye: render from the eye whose image is nearest the screen's centre axis, since that is the eye being used to look through the window. Switch to the other eye only when it is clearly nearer (by about 20 % of the interpupillary distance) for more than 300 ms. With one eye visible, that eye. Never the midpoint. | PROPOSED 2026-09-09 | Peter: "maybe the eye that is closer to the centre is the one that should matter ... If only one eye is visible then the question is irrelevant anyway." Refines D-27. |
| D-30 | Eye tracking implementation (Phase 1c): MediaPipe Face Landmarker (`@mediapipe/tasks-vision`, Apache-2.0) with the SIMD WebAssembly runtime and the `face_landmarker.task` model copied into the bundle by `scripts/fetch-assets.mjs` on install, so nothing is fetched at runtime (D-02). Camera at 640×480 from `getUserMedia`; inference on the main thread at a selectable 5–30 per second (default 20). Between fixes the eye is dead-reckoned from its last velocity (capped at 150 ms) plus the phone's inertial displacement; a lost face holds its last position for 20 s before drifting back to the default. Focal length defaults to a 95° diagonal field until calibrated against a known distance (A4 edges or 40 cm) using the median iris size over 1.5 s; camera offset from the screen centre is a constant (72 mm up for the Pixel 7a). Off-axis frustum from the eye position via a custom projection matrix; the camera moves to the eye behind the window. | DECIDED 2026-09-09 | Adds about 15 MB to the bundle. A worker would move inference off the render thread; do that only if the S8 frame-rate measurement demands it. Off by default; the panel toggle or `?eye` turns it on. |
| D-31 | Eye distance cue order: head-turn-corrected pupil spacing (63 mm) is primary when both eyes are in frame; the iris cue (11.7 mm) is continuously rescaled against it and takes over when an eye leaves the frame. Inference runs in a Web Worker; the GPU and CPU delegates are timed on the device at start and the faster kept. Focal length is stored normalised to the longest capture side (Pixel 7a default 0.76). | DECIDED 2026-09-09 | From S8 session 1: 245 ms GPU inference on the main thread, and the iris cue scaling only 19 % for a 41 % distance change. Amends D-27 and D-30. |
| D-32 | Two face models in two workers: BlazeFace short-range detector every frame for the eye centres (pupil-spacing cue at full rate), Face Landmarker at a bounded duty cycle (25 %) for iris sizes and the head-turn correction, which are then applied to the detector's spacing. Either model alone is selectable (`?model=detector|landmarker`) for S8 comparisons. | PROPOSED 2026-09-09 | Response to 250–550 ms landmarker inference on the Pixel 7a (S8). Confirm on the phone: detector ms, fix rate, and whether its eye centres are steady enough (they are coarser than iris landmarks). |
| D-32 | (status) Two-model eye tracking confirmed on the Pixel 7a with JIT allowed: detector 20 ms / 29 fixes per s, landmarker 60 ms CPU at 3–4 per s. Detector keypoint spacing scaled to the landmarker's pupil spacing by an online ratio (start 1.075). | DECIDED 2026-09-09 | S8 session 3. Supersedes the PROPOSED line above. Vanadium's per-site JavaScript JIT must be allowed for the app; add to the README and the sensor banner. |
| D-33 | The eye position is fused in the **world frame**: each camera fix is rotated into the world with the phone orientation the gyro reported at capture time, filtered there (one-euro, 0.5 Hz at rest), and rotated back into the screen frame every render frame with the current orientation. Rotating the phone about its own axes therefore changes the window geometry instantly (the picture widens or stretches as a pane of glass would), and only head motion passes through the camera's latency. Range to the eye is handled separately (dead reckoning plus the inertial bridge). Eye switching needs the other eye to be 0.6 IPD nearer the axis for 1 s; a manual left/right override exists (`?eyeside=`, panel). | DECIDED 2026-09-09 | Peter, after testing: turning the phone "has quite a bad lag ... rotating the phone along its vertical axis away from frontal should make the image on the screen wider ... That is what a piece of glass instead of my phone would do." Same intent-plus-time idea as peer dead reckoning. |
| D-29 | (amended) The viewpoint eye is chosen automatically during the first 1.5 s of tracking, then locked for the session; the panel re-chooses or pins left/right. | DECIDED 2026-09-09 | Trace session 1: every automatic switch was an unintended 8° jump. The nearest-to-axis rule still picks the initial eye. |
| D-34 | Phone **translation** is compensated as well as rotation: gravity-free acceleration is rotated into the world and integrated with zero-velocity updates (as for the throttle) into a short-horizon inertial position; each camera fix is anchored as a world position (phone position at capture plus the rotated fix), filtered there, and re-expressed in the screen frame every frame from the current orientation and position. Drift is irrelevant because only the displacement over the camera latency and filter lag (100–300 ms) is ever used. `?noinertial` disables it for comparison. | DECIDED 2026-09-09 | Peter: "maybe you should use acceleration for this, not just the gyro." Trace session 1 showed 10°/s of real eye-direction change while turning the phone by hand, which is translation the gyro cannot see. Extends D-33. |
| D-34 | (status) Parked: off by default after trace session 2 showed a 5 cm/s velocity bias in the inertial position reaching the screen as jitter. Kept behind `?inertial` / the panel with bias removal added; to be tuned offline from traces that now include raw acceleration. Rotation-only compensation (D-33) remains the default. | PROPOSED 2026-09-09 | Supersedes the DECIDED status above until the offline tuning shows a net gain. |
| D-34 | (status) **Rejected** for the PoC: simulation shows deliberate hand translations accelerate at about 0.1 m/s², below the accelerometer bias, so the integrated displacement over the camera latency is worse than not compensating. Rotation-only (D-33) stays. Code remains behind `?inertial` for a future phone with a better IMU. | DECIDED 2026-09-09 | Supersedes the PROPOSED status. Peter's idea was right in principle; the sensor is not good enough for slow motion. |
| D-35 | Orientation prediction over display latency: the orientation used for rendering is extrapolated by the last frame's angular rate over a configurable horizon (default 25 ms, `?predict=`, panel slider 0–80 ms), capped at 11°. Hand tremor at 10 Hz moves 0.5–1° during the 20–40 ms between sensor sample and photons; without prediction that shows as the scene jittering on the retina, which no eye-tracker filter can remove. | PROPOSED 2026-09-09 | Standard practice in head-mounted displays. Applies to the whole view, not only the eye. Tune the horizon by feel on the phone; overshoot on abrupt stops is the cost. Extends the intent-plus-time principle. |
